Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 14, 2026

This policy describes the Social Publisher informational website and the data the separate, self-hosted Social Publisher application is designed to process. The operator of each installation is responsible for their own installation and its handling of information.

1. Information on this website

This public GitHub Pages website provides product information and documentation. It does not ask visitors to enter Google, TikTok or other account passwords, OAuth access tokens, API keys, video files or scheduled post details. The website code does not include its own visitor analytics scripts, advertising trackers or cookie-setting scripts. The hosting provider, GitHub Pages, may collect technical access information independently; see GitHub's privacy statement.

2. Information handled by the installed application

When someone operates their own Social Publisher installation, the application may store social account identifiers and display names, authorized OAuth scopes and tokens, submitted videos and media metadata, captions, hashtags, selected account and privacy settings, scheduling information, publication status, platform-returned post IDs and technical error logs.

3. How the application uses that information

The installed application uses this information to connect accounts after authorization, validate selected media, schedule creator-directed posts, upload content to selected providers, update token sessions when permitted, display publishing status and investigate errors.

4. Storage and security

The application's database and video files are stored on the installation operator's device or server. The application encrypts stored OAuth tokens in its local database and uses a separate private owner API key for the local backend. The operator is responsible for protecting the device, encryption keys, backups, local dashboard, access permissions and any media-sharing URLs. Absolute security cannot be guaranteed.

5. Sharing with platform providers

When a creator authorizes an account or initiates a posting workflow, the application communicates with the selected provider's API, such as YouTube or TikTok. A provider may receive account identifiers, media files, post metadata and requested privacy settings necessary to complete the authorized action. Where the platform requires a publicly retrievable media URL, the media may be accessible to anyone who possesses that unguessable link while it remains enabled. The provider handles information according to its own policies.

6. Retention and deletion

Local account and posting records and submitted files remain in the installation until the operator removes them according to their own retention practices. Unlinking an account clears active authorization tokens from that account's local record; historical publishing records may be retained. The operator may also remove unnecessary media, logs and backups. Deleting local data does not remove videos already posted on an external platform, which must be managed there.

7. Your choices

You may refuse provider OAuth permissions or revoke them through a provider's account security settings. You can ask the operator of the installation you use to disconnect your account and review or delete locally retained information. The public website itself does not include an account or upload function.

8. Minors

Account authorization and publishing are intended only for people who meet each platform's age, eligibility and account-ownership requirements or who are otherwise authorized to act for that account. The public website does not intentionally solicit personal information from children.

9. Updates

We may update this policy as the application changes, or as platform or legal requirements evolve. The latest revision date will appear at the top of this page.

10. Contact and questions

For general non-sensitive questions, use the GitHub issue tracker. Issues are public, so do not disclose passwords, OAuth tokens, API keys, private media links or personal data there. For private account-related requests, contact the operator of your specific Social Publisher installation through a private channel.

Note to operator: Review this policy against your actual deployment and applicable legal obligations before submitting it to an app marketplace or for platform approval.
